Commands

You are the air traffic controller. Every command has the same shape:

plane  action  [direction]   the direction is only needed for some actions.

heron turn sturn until it flies that way: n ne e se s sw w nw
heron turn lturn 90° left · sl sr = 45° · l r = 90° · hl hr = 135°
heron descenddown to low · heron descend mid stops at mid · a level by name or number: heron descend 4 = 4,000 ft
heron ascendup as high as it goes · heron ascend mid stops at mid
heron holdcircle on the spot until you give it a turn, or land inside a landing circle · heron hold kilo flies to the fix KILO (also ALPHA, BRAVO, ZULU; NOVEMBER and SIERRA at big airports) and circles it, right turns, slowed down. Holds always stay inside your area
heron landonly inside an open landing circle (also ends a hold); a turn or hold afterwards cancels it. Above low it circles there until you descend it; at low it lines up by itself and lands, arriving at most 15° off the runway heading. With the runway busy it circles until it is free (four times round at most, then it goes around)
wren takeoff ea departure in the ready list taxis to that runway end, waits until the runway is free, takes off that way, climbs to mid and flies straight on until you turn it (you may turn it while it taxis). The guide on the right lists the ways your runways take off. When the wind leaves one way only, wren takeoff is enough
heron abortends a hold (straight on) or a landing: it goes around, climbing to mid
tutorial · speeda guided first landing, departure and hold, between shifts (skip leaves it) · time 1×, 2×, 4×, 8× (speed 4 picks one; both also buttons top right)
groundstopa favour from the shop, typed in full, used once: groundstop (no new traffic 30 s) · expedite heron (lands now) · vector (arrivals turn to their circles) · standby (no patience lost 60 s) · freeze (10 s slow motion) · cleanup (a write-up struck) · double heron (pays ×2) · tailwind (20% faster 30 s)
pausewith the Pause skills: a tactical pause, the radar stays and orders work · pause 3 stops intern screen 3 alone. Esc is the plain pause: it hides the radar and takes no orders
supervisorevery screen has its own call signs (birds, trees, rivers, stars, stones, fish): type any plane on any screen. An order to a plane an intern forgot fixes the mistake

Faded text in the prompt is a suggestion: → takes all of it, Tab takes one word. Capitals don't matter. Typos are fixed for you (nt = north): the reply shows what was understood. Esc pauses, help shows this card.

Traffic

blue planesArrivals. They come in high: bring them down to low, into a landing circle, and clear them to land.
gold planesDepartures. Each wants to leave through one of 8 slices of your area (the gold one). Take it off the runway way closest to it, then turn it that way before it reaches the edge.
helicoptersAt airports with helipads. They fly at low only and use a helipad (H), never a runway: owl land sends one to the nearest free pad (owl land india: that one), where it hovers in and sets down; kite takeoff lifts one straight up off its pad, where it hovers at low until you turn it (kite turn ne); kite takeoff ne flies that way once up. The wind does not matter to a helicopter. A helicopter keeps its pad: after landing it turns around there, then wants to leave again, so there are never more helicopters than pads (amber while taken).
purple planesOverflights from the neighbouring sector, at big airports after 10 planes. They cross in a straight line: keep them apart and let them leave through their slice.
typestold apart by the pictogram: prop slow, turns tight · jet · bizjet fast, climbs quickly · heavy and cargo big, turn wide, climb slowly, need more room, and the runway rests after them (wake) · MIL fighters (white star by the name), fast and tight-turning
priorityVIP private jets (gold star by the name) and MEDEVAC air ambulances (blue cross by the name) go first both ways: an arrival lands within 2 minutes, a departure is rolling within 50 s, not counting its taxi (the clock by its name), or it is a write-up. While one waits, other planes of its kind may not land or take off (a MAYDAY may).

Rules

crashesTwo aircraft (planes or helicopters) whose icons touch less than 400 ft apart in height collide. Close at such a height, both turn red as a warning. A storm cell, a mountain below mid and an empty tank are crashes too. A crash gets you fired (an intern is caught by the supervisor instead).
write-upsA departure leaving through the wrong slice, an arrival leaving your area, a departure waiting too long, a late priority flight, closed airspace: a write-up. Too many in one shift (3 alone, 4 · 5 · 6 with 2 · 4 · 6 screens) and you are fired. Any write-up ends your streak.
timeLevel changes take a while (bar under the label), turns are gradual, the runway takes one plane at a time (amber while busy). Everything is to scale: your area is 31 km across, a runway 3 km; time runs 7× fast; every type flies its own real speeds and turn rate.
fuelArrivals carry fuel (the jerrycan by the name, once below half): below 45 s they call MAYDAY, land them next. Departures have patience (the clock): when it runs out the flight is cancelled.
wind · stormsThe shift starts calm, then the wind comes and goes (top left). While it blows, planes take off and land into it: landing circles with the wind behind them close, a runway across the wind stays open. Helicopters ignore it, both ways. Storm cells drift with the wind and close the circles they cover.
readbackA pilot sometimes mishears: the reply shows what it understood. Say it again.
fog · nightIn fog, labels far from the airport show only the call sign; at night every label does.

Career

shift3 minutes of traffic, then the sky empties. Every plane pays Fare × Mult: its fare (less 1% per second past par), times 1 plus what your streak and tattoos add, times the airport's tier and your rank.
reviewA: no write-up, 85% on time · B: one write-up at most, 60% · C: worse. 2 A in a row: a promotion offer. 3 A in a row at one job: a bonus, cash or a rare tattoo. 2 C in a row: let go.
ranksIntern (half pay, never fired: 3 write-ups send you home; the supervisor fixes conflicts, storms and planes heading out, for a little pay) → Controller → either Senior I · II · III (one harder sector, more pay) or Supervisor · Senior Supervisor · Chief (2 · 4 · 6 intern screens: fix their mistakes).
rentDue every 3 shifts, 30% more each week, faster after week 5. Can't pay: broke, the career is over. Holding 8 weeks of rent at the end of a week: you may retire (the win).
jobsEvery week other airports want you, up to the tier your reputation (A reviews) reaches. A new job starts you over at Controller. Fired: back to intern at a small airport, cash and tattoos kept.
shopAfter every shift: kit (yours for the career) and favours (one use).
the devilEvery 2nd week: three tattoo sketches, choose one, free. Most strong ones carry a catch, some a sacrifice (a strike, your eyes, your rank, your soul). Same style on both arms or hands: 1.5× as strong. 2 and 4 of a style add its mark.
skillsMilestones give skill points; between careers they buy information and comfort (path lines, warnings, pauses, interns), never pay.
